Game Theory — An Introduction

Game theory is a branch of mathematics and economics that studies strategic interactions between rational decision-makers.

It analyzes situations where the outcome of one person’s decisions depends on the decisions of others.

In essence, it’s about predicting how individuals or groups will behave when their choices affect each other, and finding optimal strategies to achieve the best possible outcome considering all players’ actions and preferences.

It’s widely used in various fields like economics, political science, and biology, and even in everyday situations like business negotiations or social interactions.

Both players choose at the same time: Cooperate or Defect. Defecting always pays more in a single round — but if both defect, both end up worse off than if they had cooperated. That tension is the whole game.

The game is played over many rounds, so what you did last round matters. This version adds two twists from the real world: fog — sometimes you can’t see what the other side did — and noise — sometimes a move arrives flipped, so cooperation looks like betrayal.
